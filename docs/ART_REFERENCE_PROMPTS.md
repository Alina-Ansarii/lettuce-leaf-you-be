## How to keep it Papers-Please (not cutesy)

Put these words IN every prompt: *"gritty pixel art, muted desaturated palette, low resolution, chunky pixels, grounded realistic proportions, grungy weathered texture, dim moody lighting, Papers Please style, PS1-era."*
KEEP OUT of every prompt: *cute, adorable, wholesome, chibi, big sparkly eyes, glossy, clean, kawaii, bright, cheerful.* (These push it toward the look you didn't like.)

## 🧷 THE IMAGE-REFERENCE TRICK (make new dinos match Trisha)

The best way to keep every dino consistent is to **feed your actual Trisha PNG into ChatGPT as a style reference**, not just words. How:

1. In ChatGPT (the image model), **attach/upload your `trisha-neutral-png.png`** to the message.
2. Write something like:
   > "Here's my dinosaur café character in my game's art style. Make a new character in **this exact same pixel-art style, resolution, palette, and level of detail** — a [Stegosaurus / Velociraptor / etc.] café customer. Match the shading, the chunkiness, the proportions, and the outline style of the reference. [then paste the character's expression description from above]"
3. Because it can *see* Trisha, it'll match her look far better than words alone — same detail level, same vibe.
4. **Do the style test (#2) or just use Trisha as the anchor** for every subsequent character so they all feel like one set.

> This solves the "each render looks different / too cutesy" problem — Trisha becomes the visual law that all the others obey.
